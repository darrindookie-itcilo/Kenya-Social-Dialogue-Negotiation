# Kenya Social Dialogue & Negotiation Workshop

Participant dashboard · Naivasha, Kenya · 12–16 October 2026.

Open `index.html` in a browser, keeping the complete folder together. The site has no build step, external JavaScript dependencies, sign-in, analytics or server-side form collection. For hosting, publish this folder at the repository root and enable GitHub Pages from `main` / root.

The dashboard contains the five-day programme, ten opening screens (plus three optional confirmed-launch prompts), a participant activity desk, a standards library, Agreement Lab and a personal bargaining notebook with an illustrative cost calculator. Friday closes at 12:30. Times are East Africa Time (UTC+3).

Agreement Lab is a virtual card game; printing is optional. Open Agreement Lab, enter a team name if useful, and choose **Start Team Challenge** or **Open Projector Table**. Every round deals three move cards. Click or tap exactly two: they appear in the two spaces on the table. Click a selected card again, or its return button, to change your choice before revealing feedback. Write one reason; in Projector Table, a spoken reason can be confirmed instead. Select **Reveal & discuss**, then click a card to flip between its move and feedback. Continue through six rounds and export the team reflection from the results screen. Supported selected moves earn one learning point each, up to two per round; all three moves receive feedback.

Teams of 3–5 share one device per team. Projector Table is controlled by the facilitator for whole-room discussion. Each team plays its own copy; there is no live synchronization or shared leaderboard. The optional timer can start, pause, resume, add 30 seconds or reset to 90 seconds, and never locks play. Keyboard shortcuts on the table: 1/2/3 select or flip cards, F toggles fullscreen, right arrow continues after discussion, Escape exits. Shortcuts do not intercept typing in inputs. If fullscreen is unavailable, use the browser’s fullscreen control (F11 on Windows). Reset clears the saved game and team reflection after confirmation; exit preserves them. Previously saved Agreement Lab choices remain usable.

Role tabs organize fictional exercise briefs. They are not access controls: downloads in this public repository remain public. Participants should open only the brief assigned by the facilitator. No real institutional secrets should be entered into the dashboard. Notes, game progress and personal session checks save in localStorage on the current device; export before clearing or changing devices. The “Clear notes” button clears all three categories.

Service-worker caching supports the dashboard after a successful online visit. PDFs become available offline only after they have been opened or downloaded; external official links require connectivity. The complete portable ZIP includes the local resources and works without a web server.

Participant activities were adapted from the supplied workshop materials. All simulation institution labels, mandates, disputes and numbers are fictional. Full instrument PDFs include final provisions; selected treaty/recommendation pages are extracted from official ILO publications or supplied official reference appendices. Kenya statuses were checked against NORMLEX on 30 September 2026. C155 and C187 full texts are linked online. The 1966 and 1997 education instruments are Recommendations, not ratifiable treaties.

The original PowerPoints, trainer notes, scripts, facilitator master packs, ceremony run sheets, certificate templates, unpublished handbook, emails, background sources and private editorial review are excluded from this repository. The optional handbook launch screens contain general event prompts, not the unpublished handbook text. ITCILO logo is used for the requested workshop branding.

Review actual current law, worker coverage, authority, SRC advice, lawful appropriation, approvals, registration and implementation arrangements for any real agreement. The cost calculator illustrates incremental costs; it does not establish affordability or legal authority. Game points are learning prompts, not a competency assessment.
